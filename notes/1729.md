1729

"smallest number expressed as the sum of 2 positive, perfect cubes in 2 ways:
	1^3 + 12^3 = 9^3 + 10^3 = 1729

When factored, it results in three prime numbers: 7 x 13 x 19

Its divisors are: 7, 13, 19, 91, 133, 247

It is divisible by the sum of its digits:
	in base 10: 1+7+2+9 = 19; 1729/19 = 91
	
	in base 8: 3301 base 8, 3+3+0+1 = 7; 1729/7 = 247
	
	in base 16: 6C1 base 16, 6+C+1 = 19; 1729/19 = 91


The 1,729th decimal place in the number e is the beginning of the first occurance of all 10 digits w/o repetition

It is one of four "natural" numbers: 1*, 81, 1458, 1729
	When the digits of each of these numbers are added together, it produces a sum which, when multiplied by its reverse self, yields the original number
	
	1+7+2+9 = 19
	
	19 x 91 = 1729


1729 is known as the Hardy-Ramanujan number, after a famous anecdote of the British mathematician G. H. Hardy regarding a hospital visit to the Indian mathematician Srinivasa Ramanujan. In Hardy's words:
	"I remember once going to see him when he was ill at Putney. I had ridden in taxi cab number 1729 and remarked that the number seemed to me rather a dull one, and that I hoped it was not an unfavorable omen. "No," he replied, "it is a very interesting number; it is the smallest number expressible as the sum of two cubes in two different ways."
