May 10, 2005 (2 days after Mother's Day)

**_"what does your mom do?"_**

**_"a little bit of everything."_**

Mom,

Thank you. A letter that really told you how much I love you would have those two words over & over, but let's face it: it wouldn't be that interesting to read.

This last year has been a rollercoaster for me. I've changed jobs, towns, & houses. I've entered into dadhood. I've left vocational ministry and spent the better part of a year searching for God's will. Sometimes I don't know what to think. I usually put intense emotions at arm's length. I can deal with them, it's just easier not to. Over the past year I've grown tired of not living my life. Over the last year, I think I've come to a better understanding of who you and I are. Maybe I'm getting older and smarter. Maybe I'm getting older and dumber; the jury's still out on that one, but there are a few things in life that **_are_** clear.

You are amazingly encouraging! It's hard to get up each morning and go to work, not because it's boring, but because it's devoid of passion. It's hard to call out and feel like you're not being heard. when I drop Anna off in the morning, you're always there with a smile and a kind word. You tell me I can do things that sound impossibly frightening in the still of the night and utterly fantastic during the dreams of the day. I leave you encouraged and smiling. Thank you.

My daughter is a white-hot source of happiness for me. I always thought raising a child would instill fear, but somehow that has turned to joy. Her well-being is the most important thing to me right now and I deeply appreciate the time you give to watch her each week. She is an amazing source of joy, due largely in part to your constant love. At times, we've had trouble even standing on our own two feet. Your help in caring for Anna has been a life-saving act. Without you, there would be no "monkeys jumping on the bed", no "wheels on this bus", or music in the air. You are quite the "hip" grandma! Thank you.

I've read a lot of parenting books, looked through the magazines, and surfed the Internet. Your example is the source to which I keep returning. Out of all my childhood memories, happiness seems to be the dominating emotion. The amount of history between us is staggering. It's hard to comprehend or compare our length of relationship with anything else in my life. How many video tapes would our memories fill? How many episodes of Sesame Street did we watch? How many valentines did we make? How many late-night science projects did we finish? How many times did you chase me around the house with a piece of lumber? These are the questions that bleed together and overlap, adding up to one answer: thank you.

I love you on so many levels. Thank you for shaping me into who I am. Thank you for loving my wife. Thank you for caring for my daughter. Thank you for listening, giving, and encouraging. You are kind and gentle. Caring and attentive. Loving and giving. Mom and friend. Thank you.

I love you,

nate
